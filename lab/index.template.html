What to try

Move either parameter: both centers change while the projected point configurations remain projectively equivalent. Choose Cᵢ to collapse an entire parameter fiber to xᵢ. The other center travels along a rational normal curve of degree d.

S = Bld+3(P¹ × P¹),   (a,b) = f(θ,ψ).

The scroll drawings show a bounded affine window. For d > 3 they are linear shadows in three dimensions; visual crossings need not be intersections on S. The image overlay uses all coordinates for its residual, even when the display drops coordinates.

Why the raw formula misses this

At (tᵢ,sᵢ), both homogeneous coordinate vectors vanish. Different paths toward the same base point have different limiting centers. The blow-up stores the direction [ξ:η], and the resolved map is defined there.

t = tᵢ + ε cos φ,   s = sᵢ + ε sin φ.

Uncheck “On Eᵢ” and reduce ε to watch a nearby center pair approach the resolved value. At ε = 0 the viewer uses the exact first-derivative formula, rather than dividing two very small numbers.

The two missing points of Eᵢ are genuine invalid centers. Most of Eᵢ belongs to the valid locus.

Exact certificate

The two pulled-back equations vanish at every base point. We remove those factors from the eliminant before counting valid solutions. Rational polynomial division, gcd calculations, and a Sturm sequence certify the displayed counts.

The degree counts complex solutions. It does not imply that every solution is real, lies in front of a physical camera, or satisfies calibration constraints.

The exact hyperplanes use the integer configuration matrices in the downloadable certificate. They differ by projective coordinate changes from the orthonormal matrices used in the geometry viewer.

Sources and further computational directions

  • Yu, Common projection centers: Gale configurations, boundary geometry, and section algebras, accompanying revised manuscript (2026). Formulas in this lab refer to that revision.
  • Ottaviani–Thomas: the pinhole-camera common-image problem. The nearest three-dimensional geometric comparison.
  • Eisenbud–Popescu: the projective geometry of the Gale transform. The kernel viewpoint and its classical context.
  • Hartley–Zisserman: Multiple View Geometry. Projective cameras and reconstruction ambiguity.
  • Macaulay2: multigraded Betti tables. A route toward computing the unresolved resolution of R.
  • HomotopyContinuation.jl: parameter homotopies. A route toward tracking constrained solutions as the data vary.
All browser computations are local. Geometry pictures use real points and floating-point arithmetic. Hilbert and Betti tables evaluate the paper’s formulas; slice counts have separate exact rational certificates. No picture represents the entire complex surface.
